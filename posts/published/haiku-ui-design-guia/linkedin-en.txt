Give Haiku the UX rules and a checker, not just the request. The result: Sonnet-level work at about 10% of the cost.

I ran 2,244 login-screen tasks (create, improve and compare) with Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and wrote the hypotheses before running.

What worked:

→ Creating a screen: Haiku alone met 74% of the rules. With the rules in the prompt, 99%.
→ Improving a screen with hidden defects: from 75% to 89% with rules alone, and 97% with a code checker's report.
→ Cost per task: US$ 0.0022 for Haiku with the kit against US$ 0.0210 for Sonnet alone.

The rules came from open sources (WCAG, web.dev, Material Design), none from memory.

What did not work, and I say so in the article: comparing two screens, the checker did not help, and I could not measure aesthetics without a person.
