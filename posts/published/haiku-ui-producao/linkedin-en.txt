The more UX rules Haiku sees, the better it decides. But simple search is not enough.

I ran the full study, pre-registered, with open data: 2,640 calls, Haiku 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5, six families of production screens, 200 UX rules audited against their sources.

What I measured, rule by rule:

→ Without the data lake: 46% accuracy.
→ With the whole lake: 70%. With the lake plus a real-browser verifier report: 78%.
→ Accuracy climbed in steps with lake completeness: 47%, 59%, 67% and 76% on the rules shown.
→ The same amount of text about other subjects: almost no gain. The right rule is what counts.

What did NOT work, and it is in the article: retrieving rules with plain keyword search (BM25) did not keep the gain, and the verifier report made the model focus only on what it listed.

Everything is reproducible: pre-registration, deviations log, raw data and a Zenodo DOI.
